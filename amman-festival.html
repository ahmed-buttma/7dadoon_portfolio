"Hasan Mustafa, the Torchbearer"

Hasan Mustafa was a broadcaster, educator, and visionary from Battir who dedicated his life to protecting his homeland. During the 1948 catastrophe, while fear led many to flee, he refused to leave. Staying behind with just 13 men, Hasan devised a clever plan: keeping lanterns lit and laundry hanging to make the village appear fully inhabited and armed. His brave ruse scared off approaching Zionist forces, saving Battir from displacement and allowing its residents to return safely. To honor his lasting legacy, his daughter Nadia established the Hasan Mustafa Cultural Center in Battir, which played a pivotal role in having the village recognized by UNESCO. Today, Hasan Mustafa remains a timeless symbol of Palestinian rootedness and identity..

Year 2023

Freedom is Close Yet Distant

not just a photography exhibition, but a visual experience that embodies the contradiction between hope and reality, between restraint and liberation. The exhibition blends image and symbol, conveying a profound sense of the distance separating the Palestinian from their freedom, despite its apparent closeness. Through the lens, the experience reflects pain and resilience, telling stories that transcend time and geography, resonating with the hearts of those seeking to understand the meaning of stolen freedom. It is a call for reflection, to confront the truth, and to redefine the boundaries that separate us from the dream.

Year 2025

Mother of the Martyr: A Lasting Legacy

This photographic project seeks to document the personal belongings preserved by Palestinian mothers of martyrs, viewing them as living testimonies of memory, absence, and an enduring love that never fades. Through artistic and human-centered photography, the project highlights the profound bond between each mother and these possessions, which have transformed from ordinary everyday objects into powerful symbols of identity, sacrifice, and resilience. The project goes beyond portraying the steadfast, patient, and faithful image of the martyr’s mother as people commonly see her. Instead, it seeks to reveal what lies beneath the surface—what cannot be seen with the eye or expressed through words. It draws close to the hidden moments in a martyr’s mother’s life: the moments when she opens her son’s wardrobe several times a day, gazes at his clothes, touches his belongings, and relives his presence, only for the pain of loss to return quietly once again. The project demonstrates how a mother continues to carry her son within her heart, preserving the details of his life, his voice, and his memories, feeling his presence beside her in every step she takes. The work does not focus solely on loss. It also explores the inner strength of these mothers and the ways in which pain is transformed into an act of preserving memory and resisting forgetting. Through this visual narrative, the project creates a space that tells a deeply human story—one that transcends time and place.
